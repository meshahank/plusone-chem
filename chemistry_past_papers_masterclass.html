1. One-Word, Objective & Assertion-Reason Questions (1-Score)

Assertion-Reasoning (Boyle's Law) 1 Score

Question: Assertion (A): A gas having a volume of 44 L at 1 atm pressure will compress to 11 L at 4 atm pressure.
Reason (R): According to Boyle’s Law, $P_1V_1 = P_2V_2$.

Answer: Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
Explanation: $1 \text{ atm} \times 44 \text{ L} = 4 \text{ atm} \times V_2 \implies V_2 = 11 \text{ L}$.
Isoelectronic Species Identification 1 Score

Question: Identify the species among $N^{3-}, O^{2-}, F^{-}, Na^{+}, Mg^{2+}$ that has the largest ionic radius.

Answer: $N^{3-}$ (Nitride ion).
Explanation: All are isoelectronic (10 electrons each). As nuclear charge ($Z$) decreases, the attraction on electrons decreases, increasing ionic size. $N^{3-}$ has the lowest atomic number ($Z=7$), hence the largest radius.
IUPAC Nomenclature of Element 1 Score

Question: What is the IUPAC name of the element with atomic number $Z = 114$?

Answer: Ununquadium ($Uuq$).
Explanation: $1 = \text{un}, 1 = \text{un}, 4 = \text{quad} + \text{ium}$.

2. Chemical Equations, Preparations & Organic Conversions (2-3 Scores)

Hydrogenation / Reduction Equation 2 Scores

Question: Complete the reaction and name the product:
CH≡C—CH₃ + 2H₂ ⟶ ?

Answer & Equation:
CH≡C—CH₃ + 2H₂ ⟶ CH₃—CH₂—CH₃ (Propane)
Explanation: Complete catalytic hydrogenation of propyne over metal catalysts ($Pt/Pd/Ni$) reduces the triple bond to a saturated alkane.
Calcium Carbide Reaction 2 Scores

Question: Write the balanced chemical equation for the reaction of calcium carbide with water. In whose industrial preparation is this used?

Answer:
CaC₂ + 2H₂O ⟶ Ca(OH)₂ + C₂H₂ (Ethyne/Acetylene)
Explanation: This reaction is used for the laboratory and industrial preparation of ethyne gas.
IUPAC Nomenclature of Functional Groups 2 Scores

Question: Write the IUPAC name for: CH₃—CH(OH)—CH₂—COOH

Answer: 3-hydroxybutanoic acid.
Explanation: Numbering starts from the carboxylic acid carbon ($\text{-COOH}$) as C1. The hydroxyl ($\text{-OH}$) group at C3 is prefixed as 'hydroxy'.

3. Numerical Problem-Solving (3-4 Scores)

Molar Volume & Mass Calculation 3 Scores

Question: Calculate the mass and number of molecules in 112 L of $CO_2$ gas at STP.

Solution Steps:
1. Number of moles ($n$) = $\frac{\text{Given Volume at STP}}{\text{Molar Volume}} = \frac{112 \text{ L}}{22.4 \text{ L/mol}} = 5 \text{ moles}$.
2. Mass of $CO_2$ = $\text{Moles} \times \text{Molar Mass} = 5 \text{ mol} \times 44 \text{ g/mol} = 220 \text{ g}$.
3. Number of molecules = $5 \times 6.022 \times 10^{23} = 3.011 \times 10^{24}$ molecules.
Relationship between $K_p$ and $K_c$ 3 Scores

Question: Derive or write the relationship between $K_p$ and $K_c$ for the ammonia synthesis reaction:
N₂ (g) + 3H₂ (g) ⇌ 2NH₃ (g)

Solution:
Formula: $K_p = K_c(RT)^{\Delta n_g}$
• Gaseous moles of products = $2$
• Gaseous moles of reactants = $3 + 1 = 4$
• $\Delta n_g = 2 - 4 = -2$
Result: $K_p = K_c(RT)^{-2}$.

4. Reasoning & Conceptual Questions (2-3 Scores)

Dipole Moment Comparison ($NH_3$ vs $NF_3$) 3 Scores

Question: Between $NH_3$ and $NF_3$, which has a higher dipole moment? Give reason.

Answer: $NH_3$ has a higher dipole moment.
Reason: In $NH_3$, nitrogen is more electronegative than hydrogen, so bond dipoles and the lone pair dipole point in the same direction. In $NF_3$, fluorine is more electronegative than nitrogen, so the bond dipoles oppose the lone pair dipole direction, canceling out the net dipole moment.
Second Ionization Enthalpy of Sodium 2 Scores

Question: The second ionization enthalpy of sodium is much higher than that of magnesium. Why?

Answer: After losing its single valence electron, $Na^+$ attains a stable, inert-gas (Neon) electronic configuration. Removing a second electron requires disrupting this fully-filled octet core, demanding exceptionally high energy. Magnesium still has one more valence electron to easily lose.
