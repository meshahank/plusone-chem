Q1. Oxygen Atoms in $4\text{ g}$ of $O_2$
Feb 20081 Score

Question: Find the number of oxygen atoms in $4\text{ g}$ of $O_2$.

Answer: Moles of $O_2 = \frac{4}{32} = 0.125\text{ mol}$. $1\text{ mol } O_2$ contains $2 \times 6.022 \times 10^{23}$ oxygen atoms. Thus, $0.125 \times 2 \times 6.022 \times 10^{23} = 1.505 \times 10^{23}$ oxygen atoms.
Q2. Heavier Atom: One $O$ atom vs 10 $H$ atoms
Feb 20081 Score

Question: Which is heavier, one oxygen atom or 10 hydrogen atoms?

Answer: Mass of one oxygen atom = $16\text{ u}$. Mass of 10 hydrogen atoms = $10 \times 1 = 10\text{ u}$. Therefore, one oxygen atom is heavier.

Q11. Law of Conservation of Mass Explanation
March 20112 Scores

Question: State and explain the law of conservation of mass with a chemical equation.

Answer: Matter can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction. Total mass of reactants = Total mass of products. Example: $2H_2 + O_2 \to 2H_2O$ ($4\text{g} + 32\text{g} = 36\text{g}$).
Q12. Law of Multiple Proportions Statement
August 20142 Scores

Question: State the law of multiple proportions.

Answer: If two elements can combine to form more than one compound, the masses of one element that combine with a fixed mass of the other element are in ratios of small whole numbers.

Q21. Empirical to Molecular Formula Derivation
Sept 20123 Scores

Question: An organic compound gave: $C = 40\%$, $H = 6.66\%$, $O = 53.34\%$. Molecular mass is 90. Calculate its molecular formula.

Solution:
• Moles: $C = 40/12 = 3.33$, $H = 6.66/1 = 6.66$, $O = 53.34/16 = 3.33$.
• Ratio: $C:1, H:2, O:1 \implies \text{Empirical formula } = CH_2O$ (mass = 30).
• $n = 90/30 = 3$. Molecular formula = $C_3H_6O_3$.
Q22. VSEPR Theory & $H_2O$ Geometry
Model Exam3 Scores

Question: Explain the shape and bond angle of $H_2O$ molecule using VSEPR theory.

Answer: $sp^3$ hybridization, tetrahedral geometry with 2 lone pairs. Lone pair-lone pair repulsion compresses bond angle from $109.5^\circ$ to $104.5^\circ$ (bent shape).
Q23. Dipole Moment: $NH_3$ vs $NF_3$
PYQ 20223 Scores

Question: Between $NH_3$ and $NF_3$, which has a higher dipole moment and why?

Answer: $NH_3$ is higher because N-H bond dipoles reinforce the lone pair dipole, whereas F pulls electrons away in $NF_3$, opposing and canceling polarity.
Q24. Relationship between $K_p$ and $K_c$
PYQ 20233 Scores

Question: Write the relation between $K_p$ and $K_c$ for `N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃`.

Answer: $K_p = K_c(RT)^{\Delta n_g}$. For this reaction $\Delta n_g = 2 - 4 = -2$, so $K_p = K_c(RT)^{-2}$.
Q25. Quantum Numbers & Nodes for $3p$

Question: Calculate angular, radial, and total nodes for a $3p$ orbital.

Answer: For $3p$ ($n=3, l=1$): Angular = $1$, Radial = $3-1-1 = 1$, Total = $2$.

Question: Why is the second ionization enthalpy of sodium much higher than that of magnesium?

Q31. Burning of Magnesium Metal Stoichiometry
March 20094 Scores

Question: Calculate the number of moles of oxygen required to produce $240\text{ g}$ of $MgO$ by burning $Mg$ metal ($Mg = 24, O = 16$).

Solution:
• Molar mass of $MgO = 24 + 16 = 40\text{ g/mol}$.
• Moles of $MgO = 240 / 40 = 6\text{ mol}$.
• Equation: $2Mg + O_2 \to 2MgO$ ($2\text{ mol } MgO$ require $1\text{ mol } O_2$).
• Therefore, moles of $O_2$ required = $6 / 2 = 3\text{ moles}$.
Q32. Molar Mass & Volume of $CO_2$ at STP
PYQ 20244 Scores

Question: Calculate mass and total molecules in $112\text{ L}$ of $CO_2$ gas at STP.

Solution: $n = 112/22.4 = 5\text{ mol}$. Mass = $5 \times 44 = 220\text{ g}$. Molecules = $5 \times 6.022 \times 10^{23} = 3.011 \times 10^{24}$.
Q33. Limiting Reagent in Haber Process
PYQ 20214 Scores

Question: $50\text{ kg}$ of $N_2$ and $10\text{ kg}$ of $H_2$ are mixed to produce $NH_3$. Identify the limiting reagent.

Solution: Moles of $N_2 = 1786$, Moles of $H_2 = 5000$. Since $1\text{ mol } N_2$ requires $3\text{ mol } H_2$, required $H_2 = 5358\text{ mol}$. We have $5000\text{ mol}$, so $H_2$ is limiting.
Q34. Molarity Calculation ($NaOH$)
March 20114 Scores

Question: Calculate the molarity of a solution containing $8\text{ g}$ of $NaOH$ in $500\text{ mL}$ of water ($Na=23, O=16, H=1$).

Solution: Molar mass of $NaOH = 40\text{ g/mol}$. Moles = $8/40 = 0.2\text{ mol}$. Volume = $0.5\text{ L}$. Molarity = $0.2 / 0.5 = 0.4\text{ M}$.

Q36. De Broglie Wavelength Calculation
Model Exam4 Scores

Question: State de Broglie equation and calculate wavelength of an electron ($m = 9.1 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg}$) moving at $2 \times 10^6\text{ m/s}$.

Solution: $\lambda = h / mv = (6.626 \times 10^{-34}) / (9.1 \times 10^{-31} \times 2 \times 10^6) = 3.64 \times 10^{-10}\text{ m}$ ($364\text{ pm}$).
Q37. Enthalpy and Internal Energy (${\Delta H = \Delta U + \Delta n_g RT}$)
PYQ 20234 Scores

Question: Derive the relation between $\Delta H$ and $\Delta U$ for a gaseous reaction.

Solution: $H = U + PV$. For moles of gas, $PV = nRT \implies \Delta H = \Delta U + \Delta n_g RT$.
Q38. pH Calculation of Strong Base Solution
PYQ 20224 Scores

Question: Calculate the pH of a $0.01\text{ M } NaOH$ solution.

Solution: $[OH^-] = 0.01\text{ M} = 10^{-2}\text{ M}$. $\text{pOH} = -\log(10^{-2}) = 2$. $\text{pH} = 14 - 2 = 12$.
Q39. Balancing Redox Reactions by Ion-Electron Method
Model Exam4 Scores

Question: Explain the steps involved in balancing redox reactions in acidic medium using ion-electron method.

Answer: Separate half-reactions $\to$ balance atoms other than H and O $\to$ balance O using $H_2O$ $\to$ balance H using $H^+$ $\to$ balance charge using electrons $\to$ add half-reactions so electrons cancel.
