1. Physical Chemistry Numericals & Mole Concept

Molar Volume, Mass & Avogadro Number
PYQ 2024 3 Scores

Question: Calculate the mass of $112 \text{ L}$ of carbon dioxide ($CO_2$) gas at STP. Also find the total number of molecules present in it.

Step-by-Step Solution:
1. Find number of moles ($n$):
    $n = \frac{\text{Given Volume at STP}}{\text{Molar Volume}} = \frac{112 \text{ L}}{22.4 \text{ L/mol}} = 5 \text{ moles}$.
2. Calculate Mass:
    $\text{Mass} = n \times \text{Molar Mass} = 5 \text{ mol} \times 44 \text{ g/mol} = 220 \text{ g}$.
3. Calculate Number of Molecules:
    $\text{Molecules} = n \times N_A = 5 \times 6.022 \times 10^{23} = 3.011 \times 10^{24}$ molecules.
Empirical & Molecular Formula Determination
PYQ 2023 4 Scores

Question: An organic compound contains $4.07\%$ hydrogen, $24.27\%$ carbon, and $71.65\%$ chlorine. Its molar mass is $98.96 \text{ g/mol}$. Find its empirical formula and molecular formula.

Step-by-Step Solution:
1. Moles of each element (divide % by atomic mass: $C=12, H=1, Cl=35.5$):
    • Carbon: $\frac{24.27}{12} = 2.02$
    • Hydrogen: $\frac{4.07}{1} = 4.07$
    • Chlorine: $\frac{71.65}{35.5} = 2.02$
2. Simplest atomic ratio (divide each by smallest value $2.02$):
    $C: 1$, $H: 2$, $Cl: 1$ $\implies$ Empirical Formula = $CH_2Cl$.
3. Empirical formula mass = $12 + (2 \times 1) + 35.5 = 49.5 \text{ g/mol}$.
4. Find $n$ factor: $n = \frac{\text{Molar Mass}}{\text{Empirical Mass}} = \frac{98.96}{49.5} \approx 2$.
5. Molecular Formula = $n \times (\text{Empirical Formula}) = 2 \times CH_2Cl =$ $C_2H_4Cl_2$.
Limiting Reagent Problem
PYQ 2021 3 Scores

Question: 50 kg of $N_2$ (g) and 10 kg of $H_2$ (g) are mixed to produce $NH_3$ (g). Identify the limiting reagent in the production of ammonia in this reaction.

Solution:
Balanced equation: `N₂ (g) + 3H₂ (g) ⇌ 2NH₃ (g)`
1. Moles of $N_2$ = $\frac{50 \times 10^3 \text{ g}}{28 \text{ g/mol}} = 1786 \text{ mol}$.
2. Moles of $H_2$ = $\frac{10 \times 10^3 \text{ g}}{2 \text{ g/mol}} = 5000 \text{ mol}$.
3. According to stoichiometry, 1 mole of $N_2$ requires 3 moles of $H_2$.
4. For $1786 \text{ mol}$ of $N_2$, required $H_2 = 1786 \times 3 = 5358 \text{ mol}$. But we only have $5000 \text{ mol}$ of $H_2$.
Conclusion: $H_2$ is consumed first, making Hydrogen ($H_2$) the limiting reagent.

2. Inorganic Chemistry & Atomic Structure

Quantum Numbers & Orbital Nodes Calculation
PYQ 2024 3 Scores

Question: Calculate the number of angular nodes, radial nodes, and total nodes for a $3p$ orbital. State the formulas used.

Solution:
For $3p$ orbital: $n = 3$, $l = 1$.
1. Angular nodes = $l = 1$.
2. Radial nodes = $n - l - 1 = 3 - 1 - 1 = 1$.
3. Total nodes = $n - 1 = 3 - 1 = 2$.
Anomalous Configuration & Exchange Energy
PYQ 2023 2 Scores

Question: Copper ($Cu$) has atomic number 29. Write its electronic configuration and give reasons for its stability.

Answer:
Configuration: $[Ar] 3d^{10} 4s^1$ (instead of $3d^9 4s^2$).
Reasons: Fully-filled $d^{10}$ subshell provides maximum symmetrical distribution and high exchange energy, leading to exceptional stability.
Dipole Moment Comparison ($NH_3$ vs $NF_3$)
PYQ 2022 3 Scores

Question: Between $NH_3$ and $NF_3$, which has a higher dipole moment? Explain.

Explanation:
In $NH_3$, nitrogen is more electronegative than hydrogen; orbital dipole and bond dipoles reinforce each other. In $NF_3$, fluorine pulls electrons away, opposing the lone pair dipole, reducing net dipole moment. Hence, $NH_3 > NF_3$.
Molecular Orbital Theory & Bond Order ($He_2$)
PYQ 2020 2 Scores

Question: Using Molecular Orbital (MO) theory, calculate the bond order of $He_2$ and explain why it does not exist.

Solution:
Electronic configuration of $He_2$ (4 electrons): $\sigma 1s^2 \sigma^* 1s^2$
$\text{Bond Order} = \frac{N_b - N_a}{2} = \frac{2 - 2}{2} = 0$.
Since bond order is zero, the molecule is unstable and cannot exist in nature.

3. Organic Chemistry Reactions, Mechanisms & Synthesis

Markovnikov's Rule & Addition Reaction
PYQ 2024 3 Scores

Question: What happens when propene is treated with hydrogen bromide ($HBr$)? Write the chemical equation and state the rule.

Reaction:
CH₃—CH=CH₂ + HBr ⟶ CH₃—CH(Br)—CH₃ (2-bromopropane)
Markovnikov's Rule: In the ionic addition of an unsymmetrical reagent to an unsymmetrical alkene, the negative part of the reagent adds to the carbon with fewer hydrogen atoms.
Friedel-Crafts Acylation of Benzene
PYQ 2022 3 Scores

Question: Explain Friedel-Crafts acylation of benzene with chemical equation. Name the catalyst.

Solution:
Reaction of benzene with acetyl chloride in the presence of anhydrous $AlCl_3$ yields acetophenone.
C₆H₆ + CH₃COCl (anhydrous AlCl₃) ⟶ C₆H₅COCH₃ + HCl
Catalyst: Anhydrous aluminum chloride ($\text{AlCl}_3$).
Wurtz Reaction for Alkanes
PYQ 2021 2 Scores

Question: What is Wurtz reaction? Give an example for the preparation of ethane.

Answer:
Alkyl halides react with sodium metal in dry ether to give higher alkanes containing double the number of carbon atoms.
2CH₃Cl + 2Na (dry ether) ⟶ CH₃—CH₃ + 2NaCl (Ethane)
Carbocation Stability & Hyperconjugation
PYQ 2020 3 Scores

Question: Which is more stable: tertiary carbocation $(CH_3)_3C^+$ or primary carbocation $CH_3CH_2^+$? Give reasons.

Answer: Tertiary carbocation is more stable.
Reason: It possesses 9 alpha hydrogens compared to 3 in primary carbocation, resulting in greater hyperconjugative structures and electron-donating inductive effect.

4. Thermodynamics & Chemical Equilibrium

Relationship between $K_p$ and $K_c$
PYQ 2023 3 Scores

Question: Write the relation between $K_p$ and $K_c$ for the equilibrium reaction: N₂ (g) + 3H₂ (g) ⇌ 2NH₃ (g)

Solution:
Formula: $K_p = K_c(RT)^{\Delta n_g}$
Here, $\Delta n_g = 2 - (3 + 1) = -2$.
Thus, $K_p = K_c(RT)^{-2}$.
First Law of Thermodynamics
PYQ 2021 2 Scores

Question: State the first law of thermodynamics and write its mathematical equation.

Answer:
Energy can neither be created nor destroyed; total energy of an isolated system remains constant.
Equation: $\Delta U = Q + W$
